Overview

RoomieTrack is a progressive web app for students who share a home. It tracks shared expenses, splits them fairly, schedules chores and shows the shortest way to settle debts. It installs from the browser and works on phones and desktops. Try the live app.

The problem

Roommates track bills and chores in chat messages and notebooks. Costs get forgotten, chores pile up on the same person, and settling up takes many small payments. That causes arguments. I wanted one place where everyone sees the same balances and the same chore schedule.

Target users

Student roommates aged 17 to 25 who share a dorm or apartment. They log expenses, check what they owe and do their chores. They need a simple phone-first app.

Room owners who create the room, invite housemates and set up the chore schedule. A room supports up to 10 people.

My process

I worked in two-week Scrum sprints with my own backlog, so I could ship something working every sprint and drop features that did not fit the January deadline.

  1. Sprint 0Project setup, database schema, sign-in and login page
  2. Sprints 1Expense tracking, three split modes and friends
  3. Sprint 2Chores, fairness heuristic and swap requests
  4. Sprint 3Settle tab and the debt-minimization algorithm
  5. Sprint 4XP, badges, leaderboard and dark mode
  6. Sprint 5Installable app setup, rooms tab and polish
  7. Sprint 6Testing, bug fixes and documentation

Designs

I used a purple palette with Nunito and DM Sans, simple cards and a floating add button, so the main action is always in reach on a phone. Dark mode works by swapping CSS variables, so every screen themes the same way. The data model has users, rooms, expenses, expense splits, chores, chore slots and swap requests, with Row-Level Security so people only see their own room.

Decisions I made and what happened

DecisionWhyResult
Greedy debt algorithmWork out each person’s net balance, then match the biggest debtor with the biggest creditor until everyone is at zero.40% to 65% fewer transfers in my tests with 3 to 6 roommates.
Fairness heuristic for choresStart each new chore with the roommate who has the fewest slots, then rotate.Workloads stay within one slot of each other after three chores are created.
Supabase instead of my own serverAs a solo developer I needed sign-in, a database, live updates and data rules without running a backend.Housemates see changes live and can only read their own room’s data.
Installable web app, not native appsNo app store, one codebase, and it works offline for students with weak connections.Installs on Android, iOS and desktop and passed the Lighthouse PWA audit.
Plain JavaScript and CSS variablesNo build step, and a small codebase I could fully understand.Dark mode and theming work across every screen.

Final result

All planned features shipped on time: sign-in, rooms, three split modes, the Debt Eraser, chore rotation with swaps, XP and badges, dark mode and installation. I tested the main tabs, sign-in, split maths and the algorithm against manual cases with up to 6 roommates and 20 or more expenses, in Chrome, Safari and Firefox. One known gap: changes made offline do not sync after reconnecting.

Lessons learned

  • Money needs careful rounding. Decimal errors broke the balances until I added a small tolerance when comparing amounts.
  • Security rules need planning. Row-Level Security saves a lot of work, but shared rooms need carefully designed policies.
  • Caching depends on the file. Pages need fresh data, while fonts and styles can load from the cache, so I picked a strategy per file type.

Next steps: offline sync that resolves conflicts, push reminders for chores, and chore preferences in the fairness heuristic. Team work: RoomieTrack is entirely my own work. My team project is the recruitment automation below.

AI-assisted recruitment

A group project with Rylan Bernardino and Adrian Raymundo for One Source General Solutions Inc., a recruitment agency in Olongapo City. It is the topic of our BS Computer Science research paper (September 2026).

  • The problemHR staff read every Google Form response by hand, sent interview schedules by hand, and answered every applicant email themselves.
  • The AI inquiry layerGemini reads each email, works out what the applicant wants, answers routine questions from an HR-approved information sheet, and sends interested applicants the application form link.
  • People stay in chargeUnclear or sensitive emails go to an HR review sheet and trigger a Telegram alert, so a staff member decides.
  • n8n
  • Google Gemini
  • Gmail
  • Google Forms
  • Google Sheets
  • Telegram
  1. Read the emailThe workflow checks the Gmail inbox every minute.
  2. Classify the intentGemini sorts it: apply, requirements, interview question, or needs a person.
  3. Reply or flagRoutine questions get a reply. Unclear ones go to HR.
  4. Send the form linkInterested applicants get the application form. Emails with a CV skip the classifier.
  5. Screen and scheduleA rule-based workflow checks the form response and emails the exam and interview invitation.
